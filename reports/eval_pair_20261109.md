# Two new people, as the app plays: retrain and leave-two-out evaluation, 09 November 2026

*A record of that day; only this note, the marks on private documents and the `eval-pair --write` warning were updated. Since then the files dsp.py, features.py and gait.py became chain.py and walks.py, `ui.always_choose` was removed (the app always names someone), and `eval-pair` prints only unless `--write`.*

**Chosen: m1_six. M1 learned whitening on 6 features (speed, swing, q25_torso, leg_torso, moddepth_dop, sub_dispersion), trained on walks cut the way the app cuts them, with each recording's own offset removed and Room A walks added to the spread (shrink 0.5). On the app's own game (2 + 2 learning walks in the same minutes, forced choice, live walk window) it gets 87.7 % right against 78.5 % for the previous model's recipe (re-fitted per test the same way): +9.2 points, better in 15 of 15 pairs. A fully clean version of the same choice (features picked inside each test) gets 86.5 % (+8.0). Expect about +8, not +9.**

**What changed since 6 November.** The previous model (m1_core, `reports/eval_pair_20261106.md`) was trained and tested on the anchor window (first to last floor marker) with 3 learning walks each. The app instead cuts a walk from the Start click to the live arrival, and plays 2 + 2. Measured that way the previous model gets 78.5 %, not the 92 % of the old condition 1. This retrain trains on live-cut walks and tests exactly the way the app plays.

**Selection rule.** Written down in PREREGISTERED.md (private, not in this repo) on 9 Nov before any new model was scored and never changed. A procedure that chooses between options chooses inside each test fold, from leave-two-out among the four training people only. It replaces the previous model only if its nested stand score (below) beats the previous model by at least 3.0 points, AND it is not worse by more than 1.0 point on the level-matched stand score, AND it is strictly better in at least 10 of the 15 pairs. Among procedures that pass, if a simpler one is within 1.0 point of the best, the simpler one wins (m1 < m1lda < m2 < per-game discriminant < boosted trees < neural network). The previous model is re-fitted per fold the same way (m1_core, 15 features, shrink 0.3, anchor window).

Data: the six thesis participants only (P1-P6). Tests: Room B identification walks, RX2 (0.5 m beside the TX, the Open Day layout), S walks (towards the boards), days 1 and 2, natural / slow / fast; every walk went through the app's own causal chain (dsp.py, walks.py, features.py, gait.py). The walk window is the app's: from the Start click (on time, "live0", or 1 s early, "live1") to the live arrival; 174 of the 209 S walks give a usable live walk. Training may also use the training people's Room A walks (sessions 1-3, all three receivers, both directions). No home or bench recording, and no other participant, was used to fit or choose anything.

**The stand score (primary).** For each of the 15 pairs (each fit on the other four people only) and each of the 6 recordings (day x pace) where both people have at least 4 usable walks: 500 draws; in each draw 2 random walks of each person are the learning walks and every other walk of both is a game walk, given to the nearer person (forced choice, as the app with `ui.always_choose`). Averaged over draws, recordings, then pairs; the score is the mean of live0 and live1. Chance is 50 %. Intervals: 95 % bootstrap over pairs [and by person, which is wider and more honest with six people]. **Level-matched**: only pair-recordings whose two recording levels are within 3 dB (11 pairs). Secondary, never used to choose: **c2-like** (learning walks from natural pace, game walks at slow and fast pace, same day) and **c4** (learning on day 1, game walks on day 2).

## 3. Independent check

A separate check rebuilt the test from the written rules with its own code. On the same draws it gives the same numbers to the last digit (87.70 against 78.48, the same per-fold choices). On fresh draws: 87.55 against 78.41, **+9.14** (by person +5.86 to +13.06; better in 15 of 15 pairs; with any one person left out the gain stays between +7.7 and +10.6). Every possible 2 + 2 split, enumerated: 87.58 against 78.44. The clean in-fold feature choice: 86.81 / +8.40 (fresh draws, 14 of 15 pairs better) and 87.24 / +8.80 (exact). The saved model equals a from-scratch rebuild to about 1e-15 and makes the same choice in 300 of 300 games through the app's own `models.scores`. Three walks were recomputed from the raw recordings through the app's chain and matched.

**Honesty notes.** (1) **Selection on the tests.** The 6-feature set and the 0.5 shrink were found while exploring on all 15 test pairs (about 440 setups were tried there), so the headline 87.7 % / +9.2 leans optimistic, by about 1 point. The clean procedures still pass easily (FSEL +8.0 to +8.8, NS_core +7.2), and FSEL, run on all six people, picks exactly these six features. Per-recording centring alone, on the previous features and data, already gives +5.8 in all 15 pairs. (2) **Pace changes and days.** The gain is smaller and uncertain when the walker changes pace between the learning and the game walks (c2-like +3.1; by person -1.0 to +6.9; worse in 6 of 15 pairs) and across days (c4 +7.1; by person -0.2 to +13.5; worse in 3 of 15). A visitor who walks at a different pace in the game than in the learning walks is where this model gains least. (3) **Recording sensitivity.** The new model tells one person's day-1 walks from their day-2 walks at the same pace 81.7 % of the time (previous 75.2 %): it reads recording and day differences more strongly. The stand game rewards that, because two people always come from different recordings; on Open Day both walk in the same minutes with one room measurement, so part of the gain may not carry over. (4) **Six people.** The interval by person is wide (78.6 to 94.1 %): one unusual visitor can do much worse. (5) Nine Room A walks (one person, RX3) with a doubtful empty-room measurement are in the spread; they carry little weight. (6) The decision code is unchanged: the app compares a walk with the mean of each person's 2 learning walks in the learned space (`models.decide`); only the model file (and the close-call threshold in the config) changed.

## 4. Close calls: tau = 0.1043

The 15 % quantile of the new model's nested 2 + 2 live margins. With `ui.always_choose` the app still names the nearer person; tau only counts close calls in the log (15 % of decisions fall below it, and 61 % of those were right, against 93 % above it; the previous 0.0836 would put 12 % of the new model's decisions below it, and 29 % of the previous model's on 2 + 2 live walks).
